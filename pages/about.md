## About the DuBois Family Collection

Josiah E. DuBois Jr., a Camden, New Jersey attorney, oriented his career around questions of government responsibility. During World War II, DuBois worked for the United States Treasury Department and scrutinized the delays affecting aid for European Jews. Particularly, these delays were connected to a World Jewish Congress proposal to provide relief for Jews in France and Romania. The Treasury backed the granting of the required license, but the State Department stalled the proposal, concerned that the money could fall into enemy hands.

His investigation helped provide the factual basis for a damning Treasury report titled Report to the Secretary on the Acquiescence of This Government in the Murder of the Jews. The report charged the State Department with obstructing rescue initiatives and willfully stifling intelligence regarding the Nazi mass murder of European Jews. Soon afterward, President Franklin D. Roosevelt instituted the War Refugee Board. The board's primary objective was to evade government delays and respond quickly to save victims of Nazi oppression. DuBois became the agency’s general counsel and supported its endeavors to move endangered people to safety and deliver aid within Nazi-occupied Europe. 

Following Germany’s defeat in 1945, DuBois continued his work as a lead prosecutor at the Nuremberg War Crime Trials. Specifically, he oversaw the American prosecution of I.G. Farben executives, a German chemical company with an indispensable role in the Nazi war economy. The proceedings centered on the corporation’s complicity in the exploitation of camp slave labor and aggressive warfare. Through this prosecution, DuBois connected his wartime rescue efforts with his postwar pursuit of legal accountability and justice. 

The Holocaust Resource Center of Kean University, in Union, New Jersey, preserves DuBois’s archival material. The center was founded in 1982 as a partnership with Kean University and the Holocaust Resource Foundation, established by local Holocaust survivors who reconstructed their lives in New Jersey. Its public and now digitally accessible archive contains a plethora of historical papers, oral narratives, and various educational resources devoted to preserving wartime history. 
The DuBois collection contains handwritten drafts and notes regarding the formation of the War Refugee Board. Further documents pertain to Allied post-war intentions for Germany. The collection additionally contains Nuremberg trial transcripts and multiple drafts of DuBois’s memoir, documenting his experiences during and after the war. The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum in Washington, D.C., holds a separate and smaller body of related material. 

This digital repository curates primary sources on the American response to Nazi persecution and the legal proceedings of I.G. Farben executives following the Holocaust. The DuBois collection reveals the arduous process of developing policy amid deliberate bureaucratic stagnation. This conflict eventually spurred the pursuit of criminal accountability. As HRC staff continue to process the collection, students, educators, and researchers are invited to explore these primary documents firsthand. Visitors can trace how individual conviction, government policy, and the pursuit of justice intersect during one of history's darkest hours.
